Variables and operators

Most computer programs that are used in our everyday lives perform various types of calculations. For example, computer programs used by banks and supermarkets perform numerous calculations involving numbers that represent money and groceries.

Suppose you go to a supermarket and buy three pens at 300/= shillings each, a ruler for 500/= shillings and two sweets at 100/= shillings each and pay a 2000/= shillings banknote. The computer program at the supermarket shall calculate the total amount you have to pay for the items (that is, 1600/= shillings) and the change you should receive (that is, 400/= shillings). This shopping list is summarised in the table below.

Table: Shopping list

Item Quantity Unit price (shillings) Sub-total (shillings)
Pen 3 300/= 900/=
Ruler 1 500/= 500/=
Sweet 2 100/= 200/=
Total 1600/=

In this example, each purchased item (pen, ruler and sweets) is associated with two numbers: the quantities bought and the price per item. In order for the computer program to perform this calculation, a way to represent these quantities is needed. The representation of these quantities in the program is done through variables.

A computer program can have any number of variables. Each variable is identified by a name and it can hold different values while the program is running.
