Plant growth depends on the availability of essential requirements including sunlight, air, water, nutrients, and optimum temperature to grow well. The absence of any of these requirements can affect plant growth. For example, a plant may receive enough sunlight, but if it lacks water or sufficient nutrients, its growth will be affected. The essential requirements for plant growth are as follows:

Sunlight

Sunlight helps plants to grow by providing both light and heat energy. Plants use light energy to make their own food. Plant leaves contain a green pigment called chlorophyll, that absorbs sunlight and enables plants to make their own food through photosynthesis. Without adequate light, plants cannot grow well due to a lack of food.

Air

Plants use carbon dioxide gas to make their own food through the photosynthesis process. Carbon dioxide gas enters the plant through small openings on the leaves called stomata. The stomata open to allow carbon dioxide gas to enter, enabling photosynthesis to take place. Insufficient amount of carbon dioxide reduces the ability of the plant to produce its own food. Plants also need oxygen for their growth. Oxygen helps to burn food to produce the energy required for plant growth.
